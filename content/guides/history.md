## What this page is, and what it is not

This is not a history of Trenton. It is an honest inventory: the dates we can attribute to a named source, the buildings those dates attach to, and — stated plainly rather than glossed over — the parts of the story our sources simply do not contain.

That distinction matters more here than on any other page of this site. Local history is where invented facts breed, because a half-remembered date reads exactly like a researched one. Everything below either carries a source or is listed under what we do not hold.

## The short version

1. Our earliest attributed date in the town is an 1891 library building.
2. The county courthouse in use today was constructed 1903-1905, of Bedford stone.
3. A 1938 Works Progress Administration project on the fairgrounds still stands and still hosts events.
4. The twentieth-century manufacturing story we can source is thin and recent: a plant sale in 2018, alongside a closure announcement affecting 282 positions.
5. The newspaper that had covered the county since 1864 ceased in 2025 and was replaced by a new weekly that September.
6. Settlement, the townsite's earlier names, the organization of the county and the railroad era are **not** in our sources.
7. The county museum, the library and the state historical society hold the actual record. This page does not.

## The dated chronology, with sources

Each entry names who says so. Where a date rests on one source, this page says so rather than letting it borrow confidence from the entries around it.

**1857 — incorporation.** The town's tourism site refers to incorporation in 1857. Single source, uncorroborated.

**1864 — a newspaper begins.** The Missouri Press Association's account of the paper's closure describes the [Trenton Republican-Times](/place/trenton-republican-times/) as having served Trenton and Grundy County from 1864.

**1890 — a garden center.** [Barnes Greenhouses](/place/barnes-greenhouses/) gives 1890 as its establishment date on its own site. Single source, and a founding year on a business's own site is a claim rather than a record.

**1891 — the library building.** The [Grundy County Jewett Norris Library](/place/grundy-county-jewett-norris-library/) occupies an 1891 Romanesque Revival brick and limestone building listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The library's own site and an encyclopaedia entry agree.

**1898 — St. Philip's.** [St. Philip's Episcopal Church](/place/st-philips-episcopal-church/) is described as a one-storey Gothic Revival limestone building of 1898 with a clerestoried chancel, a polygonal apse and locally crafted coloured glass, on the National Register of Historic Places. Whether the congregation is currently active is not something our sources establish — only the building is documented.

**1903-1905 — the courthouse.** The [Grundy County Courthouse](/place/grundy-county-courthouse/) was constructed between 1903 and 1905 of Bedford stone, per the county's own buildings-and-grounds page. It still houses the clerk, collector-treasurer, assessor, commission and circuit court offices — a dated structure that is also a working office.

**1908 — a golf club.** [Riverside Country Club](/place/riverside-country-club/) gives 1908 as its founding year, on ground along the Grand River.

**1919 — a bank.** [BTC Bank](/place/btc-bank/) describes itself as a community bank established in 1919.

**1929-1930 — a downtown hotel building.** A five-storey Art Deco building of 1929-1930, originally a hotel, was added to the National Register in 2001 and has been converted to apartments. It appears in our research and **not** in our lodging records, deliberately: a traveller who tries to book a room in an apartment building has been misled, and several directories still make exactly that mistake.

**1938 — the Rock Barn, and the Chamber.** The octagonal limestone Rock Barn at the [North Central Missouri Fairgrounds](/place/north-central-missouri-fairgrounds-rock-barn/) was built in 1938 as a Works Progress Administration project and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The [Trenton Area Chamber of Commerce](/place/trenton-area-chamber-of-commerce/) gives the same year, 1938, as its formation. Two unrelated institutions, one year — a coincidence rather than a connection, and we make no claim of one.

**2018 — the plant.** [The Trenton plant](/place/nestle-trenton-plant/) was acquired by Nestlé from Conagra Brands in 2018. Under Conagra it was reported as the town's largest employer and produced canned meat products, and Conagra had announced a closure eliminating 282 positions before the sale. **All of that is 2018 reporting.** We assert nothing about the plant's operations, ownership or headcount today.

**2025 — the paper, and its replacement.** The Republican-Times closed after its June 19, 2025 issues. [The Trenton Telegraph](/place/trenton-telegraph/) launched that September under county industrial development ownership. See [how to follow local news](/guides/local-news/).

## What our sources do not contain

A real history of this place would open with things we do not hold, and pretending otherwise would be the exact failure this site exists to avoid. We have **no sourced account** of:

- when the area was settled, or by whom;
- what the townsite was called before it was called Trenton, or why the name changed;
- when and how Grundy County was organized, or the origin of its name;
- when Trenton became the county seat, or whether that was ever contested;
- the arrival of the railroad, which line it was, or what it did to the town's shape and economy;
- the manufacturing arc across the twentieth century, of which we hold only its final decade.

That list is not decoration. It is the working brief for whoever fills this page in properly, and every item on it is answerable from records that exist — just not from search results.

## Where the record actually is

**[Grundy County Museum](/place/grundy-county-museum/).** Several buildings, including the county's only remaining one-room schoolhouse, with exhibits of Native American artefacts, antique vehicles and a Black history display. It runs a season — the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend through the third Sunday in October, per the museum's own contact page — so it is not a year-round research option.

**[Grundy County Jewett Norris Library](/place/grundy-county-jewett-norris-library/).** The public library for the town and county.

**The Recorder's office** at the [courthouse](/place/grundy-county-circuit-clerk-and-recorder/), for recorded documents and circuit court records.

**The State Historical Society of Missouri and the National Park Service's own register**, for county collections and for the definitive list of registered places. The town's tourism site refers to seven registered places in the Trenton area; we can name only some, which is why we present names rather than a list.

## A note on marker text

Where a date on this page traces to a plaque, a marker or a promotional page, it is marker text and not the historical record. Markers are written by people with an interest in the story, sometimes decades after the events, and they are corrected far less often than they are photographed. Treat them as a lead into the archive rather than as the archive.

## What we could not confirm

Every date above, in the sense this site means it: we have not opened a single one of the pages we cite, and no one has consulted a primary record. Two entries rest on a single source, and one — the 1857 incorporation date — rests on a promotional page. If you have documentary evidence for anything in the gap list, [tell us](/submit/), and we will publish it with your source named.
