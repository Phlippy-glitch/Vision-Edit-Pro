## Who this is for

You want to know what is actually happening in Trenton and Grundy County — a school board decision, a road closure, a parade route, a fair date — and you want to know which sources are live right now.

That last part is the whole point of this page. Trenton's news landscape changed in 2025, and any directory built before then, or built carelessly after, will point you at a publication that no longer publishes.

## The short version

1. [KTTN / KGOZ radio](/place/kttn-kgoz-radio/) is the working broadcast news outlet for the town and county.
2. [The Trenton Telegraph](/place/trenton-telegraph/), a weekly, launched in September 2025 and is the town's current newspaper.
3. The [Trenton Republican-Times](/place/trenton-republican-times/) closed after its June 19, 2025 issues. It is history, not a news source.
4. Its former domain still resolves and still ranks in search. Do not mistake that for a live outlet.
5. For civic decisions, the [City of Trenton](/place/city-of-trenton/), the [County Clerk](/place/grundy-county-clerk/) and the [school district](/place/trenton-r-ix-school-district/) publish their own notices.
6. For downtown events, [Main Street Trenton](/place/main-street-trenton/) and the Chamber are the organizers, and organizers are more current than any calendar that copies them.
7. Cross-check anything time-critical against two of the above before you act on it.

## The outlets that are live

**[KTTN / KGOZ radio](/place/kttn-kgoz-radio/).** A three-station group based in Trenton covering north Missouri — KTTN-FM 92.3, KTTN-AM 1600 and KGOZ-FM 101.7 — with local newscasts described as airing six times a day and sportscasts three times a day, per the station's own contact page.

Worth saying plainly, because it is the sort of thing a directory usually flattens: in our research this was the outlet doing the routine local reporting. Fair dates, park board decisions, the newspaper transition itself — the usable secondary coverage of this county came from here more than from anywhere else. If you follow one source, follow this one.

**[The Trenton Telegraph](/place/trenton-telegraph/).** A weekly newspaper for Trenton and Grundy County launched in September 2025, owned by the [Grundy County Industrial Development Corporation](/place/grundy-county-industrial-development-corporation/) with CherryRoad Media providing management. Its own description covers schools and youth sports, town government, community events and business news.

One deliberate omission: our sources give both September 11 and September 12, 2025 as the launch day, so we publish the month and not the day. A one-day discrepancy is trivial in itself and is a useful demonstration of the rule — where two publishers disagree, we print the part they agree on.

**First-party civic sources.** For meetings, notices, elections and closures, the organization itself publishes before anyone reports it: the [City of Trenton](/place/city-of-trenton/) for municipal matters, the [Grundy County Clerk](/place/grundy-county-clerk/) for elections and polling places, the [Trenton R-IX district](/place/trenton-r-ix-school-district/) for school calendars and board business, and [North Central Missouri College](/place/north-central-missouri-college/) for campus and athletics events.

**Event organizers.** [Main Street Trenton](/place/main-street-trenton/) for downtown events and the market, the [Trenton Area Chamber of Commerce](/place/trenton-area-chamber-of-commerce/) for the Missouri Day Festival. Organisers change dates; calendars that copy organizers do not always follow.

## The outlet that is not live, and why it matters

The [Trenton Republican-Times](/place/trenton-republican-times/) served Trenton and Grundy County from 1864 until it was closed after the June 19, 2025 issues. It no longer publishes. We list it because a county's newspaper of that duration is part of the record, and we list it as closed.

**Its website field on this site is deliberately empty.** The former domain still resolves and still ranks well in search results, which is the trap: a reader who lands there sees a newspaper-shaped site and reasonably concludes the paper is running. A directory that links to it as a live outlet has not merely made a small error — it has taken the one question this page exists to answer and answered it wrong.

This is worth being explicit about, because it is the failure that kills trust in local directories generally. Listing a business that closed sends someone on a wasted drive. Listing a dead newspaper as a live news source sends them to look for information that will never appear, about a place they are trying to understand. A stale listing does not read as stale; it reads as authoritative until the moment it fails.

## Our own working, shown

Since this page is about outlets getting things wrong, here is exactly what we did and did not do.

We hold two sources on the closure — a state press association account and local radio coverage — and two on the launch of the replacement, one of them the new paper itself. Both passes of our research independently flagged the old domain as a hazard. We recorded the paper's status as closed rather than unknown, and we suppressed its website field at the data level so that no template can render it as a link, no matter how the record is reused later.

What we did **not** do: open any of those pages, call either publication, or check whether the new weekly is still publishing today. Our record of the launch is a 2025 report, and 2025 reports age. If the Telegraph's situation has changed since, we would not know, and this page would be wrong in the same category of way it is warning you about. That is not a comfortable sentence to write on a page like this one, which is why it is here.

## How to check something time-critical

1. Go to the organization itself first — city hall, the county, the district, the organizer. A meeting notice originates there.
2. Check the radio group's coverage for anything decided rather than scheduled.
3. Check the weekly for the fuller account, remembering that a weekly's news is up to seven days old by the time it reaches you.
4. If two sources disagree on a date or a time, treat the organizer as correct and the reprint as stale.
5. If it is genuinely urgent — a closure, an outage, an emergency — call the responsible office rather than reading anything, including us.

## What we could not confirm

Whether any outlet named here is publishing or broadcasting on the day you read this; the current ownership or management arrangements of the weekly; the frequencies, schedules and newscast counts described on the radio group's own page. None of the pages cited here has been opened by us, and none of the publications has been contacted. If an outlet has changed, [tell us](/submit/) — of every page on this site, this is the one where being out of date is self-refuting.
