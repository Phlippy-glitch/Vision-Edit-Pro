## Who this is for, and how to read it

You want to know what a year here looks like — when to visit, when to avoid, when the thing you are counting on is closed. This page is written as **rules, not dates**: "third weekend of October" rather than a date that will be wrong within a year.

It is not an events calendar, and it will not tell you what is on next Saturday. Where our sources gave conflicting dates, we print none and say so — two of the town's larger events fall into that category. Where a single source did give dates for one year, you will find them on that event's own page, framed as what to check rather than what to count on.

## The short version

1. **May through September, Saturday mornings** — the farmers market in [Sesquicentennial Park](/place/sesquicentennial-park/).
2. **Mid-March to mid-October** — the seasonal garden center.
3. **Memorial Day weekend Saturday to the third Sunday in October** — the [county museum's](/place/grundy-county-museum/) season.
4. **March to November** — the campground season at Crowder State Park, with services on a shorter window.
5. **Summer** — the municipal aquatic center, on dates set annually by the Park Board.
6. **Mid-July** — a music and camping festival at the winery.
7. **Late July into early August** — the North Central Missouri Fair. Dates disputed; check first.
8. **Third weekend of October** — the Missouri Day Festival, the town's largest weekend.
9. **First Friday in December** — the downtown Christmas event, under at least four different names.

## Spring

**Mid-March.** [Barnes Greenhouses](/place/barnes-greenhouses/) describes a season running mid-March through mid-October. In a farming county, the garden center opening is a real marker of the year rather than a retail one.

**March.** Missouri State Parks describes the [Crowder State Park campground](/place/crowder-state-park-campground/) season as March through November, with on-season services reported from April 15 to October 31. The gap between those two windows is where an early-season camper gets caught. See our [park guide](/guides/crowder-state-park/).

**Late May.** The [Grundy County Museum](/place/grundy-county-museum/) opens for its season on the Saturday of Memorial Day weekend, per its own contact page, and runs weekends and holidays through the third Sunday in October. Admission is described as free with donations accepted. Outside that window the museum is not an option, which catches visitors who assume a museum is a year-round thing.

**May.** [Main Street Trenton](/place/main-street-trenton/) opens the Saturday morning producer market in [Sesquicentennial Park](/place/sesquicentennial-park/), described as running May through September with vegetables, fruit, flowers, eggs, honey, baked goods and handmade items.

## Summer

**Summer, dates set annually.** The [Trenton Family Aquatic Center](/place/trenton-family-aquatic-center/) inside Moberly Park is a summer facility. Its season dates, hours and admission are set each year by the Park Board and were not established in our research — which is precisely why this page tells you to call the Park Department rather than showing you last year's dates.

**Mid-July.** The Mid-America Music Festival, described by its organizers as a two-day country music and camping festival on the grounds of [Black Silo Winery](/place/black-silo-winery/), with food vendors, a wine tent, beer stations and camping. It is described as the main fundraiser for the Black Silo Foundation. Ticketing, parking and camping are handled on the festival's own site.

**Late July into early August.** The North Central Missouri Fair at the [fairgrounds](/place/north-central-missouri-fairgrounds-rock-barn/): livestock competitions and animal shows, a carnival, live music, a parade, motorsports and grandstand events. **We publish no dates.** Three different date spans appeared across our research for overlapping years, and no official website was found — only a social page. For a multi-day event that people book time off for, that is a gap worth naming rather than papering over.

## Autumn

**August.** The school year begins. We hold no calendar and publish none; the [Trenton R-IX](/place/trenton-r-ix-school-district/) district site is the authority, and the calendar is the single most-consulted document of any local family's year. [North Central Missouri College](/place/north-central-missouri-college/) runs its own academic calendar, which does not have to match the district's.

**Autumn generally.** Hunting is part of the year here — the town's own tourism material leans on it — and every date attached to it is set by the state conservation agency rather than locally. Take seasons, permits and regulations from that agency directly. We publish none.

**Third weekend of October.** The **Missouri Day Festival**, organized by the [Trenton Area Chamber of Commerce](/place/trenton-area-chamber-of-commerce/) at the [North Central Missouri Fairgrounds](/place/north-central-missouri-fairgrounds-rock-barn/): a parade, a statewide high school marching band competition, children's activities, demonstrators, and craft, antique and food vendors. The parade is reported to run Saturday morning at Main and 9th Streets, sponsored by the [Rotary Club of Trenton](/place/rotary-club-of-trenton/).

Three cautions on this one, all of them things a visitor gets wrong. The name is **Missouri Day**, singular — a separately named Missouri Days Marching Festival exists and the two must not be merged. The duration has varied between two and four days by year, so do not assume a Saturday-and-Sunday shape. And we print **no vendor count**, because four different figures appear across our sources; a number that unstable tells you only that publishers are copying each other.

**Third Sunday in October.** The museum season ends, and the garden center's season closes around the same time. Mid-October is where the town's warm-weather offering shuts down over about two weeks.

## Winter

**First Friday in December.** The downtown Christmas event, organized by [Main Street Trenton](/place/main-street-trenton/) and [Five Points Alive](/place/five-points-alive/), described as including a lighted parade, a gingerbread and cookie contest, a soup supper, live music, holiday musical presentations and a Santa appearance at the Sesquicentennial Park gazebo. The parade is reported to form at Farmers Bank of Northern Missouri and to stop at the town's nursing homes before reaching downtown — which is the detail on this page most worth knowing, and the one no aggregator will ever carry.

Two honest caveats. Our sources use at least four names for this event, and it is unresolved whether this is one event or an umbrella over several named components. And one report gives a December 1 parade date that does not fall on a first Friday, so the rule and the reporting disagree. We publish the rule and no dates.

**January to March.** The quiet stretch. Seasonal businesses are shut, the park is open but the campground services are not, and the school and college calendars carry most of what happens. This is the part of the year promotional pages omit, and omitting it is how they mislead people choosing when to visit.

## What we could not confirm

Every date, every season boundary and every recurrence rule on this page. We have opened none of the pages we cite. The fair's dates and the December event's name and date are actively conflicted across our sources; the aquatic center's season is set annually and unknown to us; the school and hunting calendars we deliberately do not hold. Before you travel for any of these, check the organizer. If you spot a rule we have wrong, [tell us](/submit/).
