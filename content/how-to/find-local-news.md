## The short answer, and the trap

**The newspaper most search results still point you at is not publishing.** The Trenton Republican-Times, which had served Trenton and Grundy County since 1864, closed after its June 19, 2025 issues. Its former domain still resolves and still ranks well, and nothing about a newspaper-shaped page announces that the newspaper has stopped.

The outlets to use instead: **[KTTN / KGOZ radio](/place/kttn-kgoz-radio/)** for day-to-day coverage of the town and county, and **[The Trenton Telegraph](/place/trenton-telegraph/)**, a weekly launched in September 2025, for the fuller written account.

## The outlets that are live

**Radio.** KTTN describes itself as a three-station group based in Trenton covering north Missouri — KTTN-FM 92.3, KTTN-AM 1600 and KGOZ-FM 101.7 — with local newscasts airing six times a day and sportscasts three times a day, per the station's own contact page. In our research this was the outlet doing the routine local reporting: fair dates, board decisions, the newspaper transition itself. If you follow one source, follow this one.

**The weekly.** [The Trenton Telegraph](/place/trenton-telegraph/) launched in September 2025, owned by the [Grundy County Industrial Development Corporation](/place/grundy-county-industrial-development-corporation/) with CherryRoad Media providing management. Its own description covers schools and youth sports, town government, community events and business news. Note what a weekly means for timing: by the time news reaches you, it can be up to seven days old.

One deliberate omission: our sources give both September 11 and September 12, 2025 as the launch day, so we publish the month and not the day. A one-day discrepancy is trivial in itself and is a useful demonstration of the rule — where two publishers disagree, we print the part they agree on.

**The bodies themselves.** For meetings, notices, closures and elections, the organization publishes before anyone reports it — the [City of Trenton](/place/city-of-trenton/) for municipal matters, the [Grundy County Clerk](/place/grundy-county-clerk/) for elections and polling places, [Trenton R-IX](/place/trenton-r-ix-school-district/) for school calendars and board business.

**Event organizers.** [Main Street Trenton](/place/main-street-trenton/) for downtown events and the market, and the Chamber for the town's festival. Organizers change dates; calendars that copy organizers do not always follow.

## The transition, in order

- **1864** — the [Trenton Republican-Times](/place/trenton-republican-times/) begins serving Trenton and Grundy County, per the Missouri Press Association's account of its closure.
- **June 19, 2025** — the paper's final issues. After 161 years, the county's newspaper of record stops publishing.
- **September 2025** — the Trenton Telegraph launches as a weekly replacement, under county industrial development ownership with outside management.
- **Since then** — the old paper's domain keeps resolving and keeps ranking, which is why the trap on this page outlives the closure itself.

Any directory built before 2025, or built carelessly after, will point you at a publication that no longer publishes. That is the single fact this page most needs you to carry away.

## The one to be careful with

We list the [Trenton Republican-Times](/place/trenton-republican-times/) because a county newspaper of that length of run is part of the record. We list it as closed, and **its website field on this site is deliberately empty**, so no template can render it as a live link no matter how the record is reused later.

Why this matters more than a normal stale listing: a closed shop sends someone on a wasted drive, which is bad. A dead newspaper presented as a live news source sends someone looking for information that will never arrive, about a place they are trying to understand — and they will not realize it for weeks. A stale listing does not read as stale; it reads as authoritative until the moment it fails.

## Our own working, shown

Since this page is about outlets getting things wrong, here is exactly what we did and did not do. We hold two sources on the closure — a state press association account and local radio coverage — and two on the launch of the replacement, one of them the new paper itself. Both passes of our research independently flagged the old domain as a hazard, and we recorded the paper's status as closed rather than unknown.

What we did **not** do: open any of those pages, call either publication, or check whether the new weekly is still publishing today. Our record of the launch is a 2025 report, and 2025 reports age. If the Telegraph's situation has changed since, we would not know, and this page would be wrong in the same category of way it is warning you about. That is not a comfortable sentence to write on a page like this one, which is why it is here.

## How to check something time-critical

1. Go to the responsible body first. A meeting notice, a closure or a deadline originates there.
2. Check the radio group for anything already decided rather than merely scheduled.
3. Check the weekly for the fuller account, allowing for its publication cycle.
4. If two sources disagree on a date or a time, treat the organizer as authoritative and the reprint as stale.
5. If it is urgent — a road closure, an outage, an emergency — call the responsible office rather than reading anything, this site included. For an emergency, call 911.

## What to ask, if you are calling around

1. Where do you publish your meeting agendas and notices, and how far ahead?
2. Is there a mailing list or an alert I can sign up for?
3. Who do I contact to submit an event, an obituary or a correction?
4. Is there an archive of the closed newspaper, and where is it held?

Question four is worth asking at the [library](/place/grundy-county-jewett-norris-library/). Newspaper archives are a common county-library holding, and a run beginning in 1864 is the kind of record that outlives the publication.
