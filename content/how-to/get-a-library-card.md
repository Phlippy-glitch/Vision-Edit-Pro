## The library, and what to take with you

**[Grundy County Jewett Norris Library](/place/grundy-county-jewett-norris-library/) is the public library for Trenton and Grundy County.** Opening a card is usually a single counter visit; the whole task is arriving with the right two documents.

Take **photo identification** and **something showing your current address** — an addressed piece of mail, a lease, a utility statement. If the card is for a child, expect a parent or guardian to need to be there to sign for it. If you live in the county rather than the city, or outside the county entirely, ask what applies to you before you go; residency is the thing that changes the terms.

## Before you drive: the hours, and the lookalike site

We hold **conflicting information about the Saturday closing time**, so we print no hours at all for this library. That is not an oversight. Call, or check the library's own page, before you plan a Saturday around it.

There is also a name trap worth knowing. The correct address for this library online is **grundycountylibrary.org**. A similarly named site belonging to a library in a **different Trenton** turns up in searches, and it will hand you the wrong catalog, the wrong hours and the wrong phone number with complete confidence. Check the domain before you trust the page.

## What a card usually unlocks

Ask what this library offers rather than assuming from another town's list, but the questions worth asking are consistent:

- **Borrowing limits and loan periods**, and how renewals work.
- **Interlibrary loan.** If a book is not on the shelf here, a small library can usually get it from elsewhere — with a lead time. This is the service that most changes what a small-town card is worth, and the one people most often do not know they have.
- **Digital access.** Ebooks and audiobooks, and often magazines or streaming, through platforms your card number logs you into from home.
- **Computers, wifi and printing**, including what printing costs and whether you can send a job from your phone.
- **Meeting or study space**, and whether it can be booked by the public.
- **Programs** for children, teenagers and adults, including anything that runs over the summer.
- **Local history and genealogy material.** County libraries frequently hold records that exist nowhere online, which matters if you are researching a family or a property here.

## What goes wrong

**Arriving without proof of address.** The commonest reason a card visit becomes two visits.

**Assuming a child can get their own card unaccompanied.** Usually not, and it is a wasted trip for a teenager sent alone.

**Expecting an interlibrary loan to arrive this week.** Ask for the realistic timeline and work back from when you need it.

**Fines and lost items.** Ask what the policy is now — many libraries have changed theirs — and ask what happens to a card with something outstanding on it, because that is what blocks a borrow later.

**Following the wrong domain.** See above. It is the single easiest mistake to make about this library.

## What to ask when you call

1. What do I need to bring to open a card, and does that change if I live outside the city or the county?
2. Is there a fee for anyone, and if so, for whom?
3. What can I use with the card from home, and how do I log in?
4. How does interlibrary loan work here, and how long does it usually take?
5. What are your hours this month, including Saturday?

Question five is the one to ask out loud rather than read anywhere, including here.
