## The office you want is the county's, not the town's

**This page says Grundy County rather than Trenton because property tax is a county matter: a Trenton address declares property to, and pays taxes at, county offices — not City Hall.** Both of those offices sit in the [Grundy County Courthouse](/place/grundy-county-courthouse/) at 700 Main Street in Trenton, which the county's own pages describe as housing the clerk, collector-treasurer, assessor, commission and circuit court offices.

This page prints no rate, no due date, no fee and no office hour. None has been checked with either office, and a wrong figure on a tax page costs someone a penalty. What it gives you is the two offices, the order between them, and the questions that produce real numbers.

## The short version

1. Two offices matter, and they are not interchangeable: the [Grundy County Assessor](/place/grundy-county-assessor/) values property, and the [Grundy County Collector-Treasurer](/place/grundy-county-collector-treasurer/) collects the taxes.
2. Personal property — vehicles and the like — is declared to the Assessor, which the county's own page describes as maintaining the list of taxable real and personal property in Grundy County.
3. The Collector-Treasurer collects real estate and personal property taxes and serves as county treasurer, per the county's own page.
4. The same office issues the paid-tax receipt that vehicle licensing requires — which is why this errand blocks that one.
5. Due dates: ask the Collector-Treasurer. We print none, and a date from anywhere other than that office is not worth acting on.
6. New to the county? Ask the Assessor what a new resident declares, and by when, before anything else.

## Assessment first, collection second

The order is what catches people. The Assessor maintains the list of taxable real and personal property — its office is where property is declared and where an assessment is questioned. The Collector-Treasurer then collects the taxes levied on what the Assessor listed.

Two consequences follow. If you think a bill is wrong because the *valuation* is wrong, the Collector-Treasurer cannot fix it — that is an Assessor conversation, and it has its own calendar. And if you were never assessed here, there is nothing for the collector to bill and no receipt for it to issue — which surfaces months later, at vehicle-licensing time, as a missing document. Ask the Assessor's office what it issues to a new resident instead of a paid-tax receipt; that is question one of a move here, not question three.

## What to bring, or have in front of you

- The property address, and any parcel or account identification from a previous bill.
- For personal property, a list of what you owned on the date the office asks about — vehicles are the usual case.
- The bill itself, if one has arrived. It carries the account detail that makes the call or the counter visit short.
- If this is feeding vehicle licensing: ask specifically for the paid receipt, and keep it. That piece of paper is the point of the errand.
- A payment method the office accepts — ask what is accepted before you go rather than at the counter.

## What to ask when you call

1. What do I owe on this parcel or account this year, and when is it due?
2. What does a new resident declare, to which office, and by what date?
3. If I disagree with a valuation, what is the process, and what is its deadline?
4. What happens if a payment is late, and what does it take to bring an account current?
5. What exactly do I need from you for vehicle licensing, and is it issued on the spot?

## If something goes wrong

A disputed valuation goes to the Assessor. A payment, receipt or balance question goes to the Collector-Treasurer. Both offices are in the [courthouse](/place/grundy-county-courthouse/), so the wrong-office penalty here is a walk down a hallway rather than a second trip. For the errand this receipt usually feeds, see [licensing a vehicle](/how-to/license-your-vehicle/) — and note that the license office itself is a separate, state-contracted matter with an open question attached.
