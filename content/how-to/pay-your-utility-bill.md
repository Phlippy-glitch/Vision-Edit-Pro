## Start with the statement in your hand

**The amount, the due date and where to send it are printed on the bill. Use the bill.** This page is for what the bill does not explain: who to call, what they ask for, and the mistakes that cost people money.

We print no figure here — no fee, no due date, no penalty, no counter hours. Nothing on this page has been checked with the utility, and a plausible number that turns out wrong is worse than none. What follows is the shape of the task and the questions that get the real numbers in one call.

## First: which utility is yours

Inside Trenton city limits, the City of Trenton's billing pages describe [Trenton Municipal Utilities](/place/trenton-municipal-utilities/) as the city-owned provider of electric, water and wastewater service. Outside them, [Grundy Electric Cooperative](/place/grundy-electric-cooperative/) describes itself as a member-owned cooperative for rural members in northern Missouri and southern Iowa.

Do not infer this from your postal address. A Trenton, MO 64683 mailing address is not the same thing as an address inside city limits, and the boundaries need not agree. Ask the utility to check the exact address.

## Have your account number in front of you

Every route asks for it before anything else, and an automated phone line will not get past its first prompt without it. It is on the statement. If you pay for more than one property, each has its own number, and the digits are easy to transpose. Have the service address ready too — billing and service addresses are different fields, and a payment posted to the wrong one is slow to unwind.

## The routes, and what we can tell you about each

A city-run utility typically offers some mix of an online portal, an automated phone line, a counter, mail and an after-hours drop box. Our sources name a municipal online payments portal for the City of Trenton, linked below. Which other routes Trenton offers, what each costs and when the counter is staffed are held on the utility's own pages, and we link out rather than paraphrase. A paraphrased fee is an invented fee.

Two rules hold whichever route you choose. **Never put cash in a drop box** — use a cheque or a money order so a record exists. And if you pay by card, ask whether the processor adds a convenience fee *before* you confirm, not after it appears on your statement.

## What catches people is the penalty, not the bill

Municipal utility penalties are usually a flat percentage added on a fixed day of the month, and they are usually not prorated: one day late costs what ten days late costs. Trenton's day and Trenton's percentage are printed on your statement. We do not print either, and we will not guess.

The second trap is disconnection. Disconnection and reconnection are separate processes with separate costs, and the reconnection cost is the one people learn about too late. Ask while your account is in good standing, not on the day the power goes off.

## What to ask when you call

Account number in front of you, then, in this order:

1. What day is payment due, and what happens when that day falls on a weekend or a holiday?
2. What is the late penalty, and is it charged on the whole bill or only part of it?
3. Is a deposit required for new service, and when is it refunded?
4. How long after a missed payment does disconnection begin, and what does reconnection cost?
5. Are trash and recycling billed on this account, and can either line be removed?

Question five surprises new residents most. The City's pages describe residential collection as contracted out — currently reported as [Rapid Removal Disposal](/place/rapid-removal-disposal/) for trash and [Hope Haven Industries](/place/hope-haven-industries/) for recycling — with residents billed through the utility. The office you would call to argue about the charge may not be the office that set it. Ask who sets it before asking to have it removed.

## If it goes wrong

A billing dispute goes to the utility office. A service problem — no water, no power, a leak — is a different call and a different page: [reporting a water or power problem](/how-to/report-a-water-or-power-problem/). The contract behind a trash line is a [City of Trenton](/place/city-of-trenton/) matter, not a hauler one.
