## The office you want is the County Clerk

**Voter registration for a Trenton address is handled by the [Grundy County Clerk](/place/grundy-county-clerk/), in the [Grundy County Courthouse](/place/grundy-county-courthouse/).** Registration is a county function, not a city one, so City Hall is the wrong building for this errand.

This page prints no deadline, no fee and no office hour. None has been checked with the clerk's office, and a wrong date here would cost someone a vote. What it gives you is the sequence, what to carry, and the questions that settle everything else in one call.

## What to have with you

- **Proof of identity.** Expect to be asked for a Missouri driver license or state ID number, or the last four digits of a Social Security number.
- **Your current residential address**, including any apartment or unit. This is the field that assigns your polling place, and a mailing address is not always the same thing.
- **Your former address**, if you have moved. It lets the clerk find and close the old registration rather than leave two.
- **Your former name**, if it changed. Same reason.
- Time to read the form before signing. A registration rejected for a missing field takes a second trip.

## The order this goes in

1. Register, or update your existing registration, with the County Clerk.
2. Confirm which polling place your address feeds — and confirm again after any move, however short.
3. Before each election, check what identification you will be asked for at the polls, and what the process is if you do not have it with you.
4. If you cannot vote in person, ask which absentee or mail-in route applies to your situation and what its own, earlier, deadline is.
5. Keep the acknowledgement the clerk sends you. It is the fastest way to resolve a dispute at a polling place.

## The deadlines that actually catch people

**Registration closes before election day.** The most-missed civic deadline anywhere, and the one to ask about first. It is not "before the election" in any loose sense — there is a specific cutoff date, and the office can tell you what it is for the election you have in mind.

**Absentee and mail routes close earlier still,** and they usually involve a request deadline *and* a return deadline. Ask about both. A ballot requested in time and returned late counts for nothing.

**Moving is a registration event.** People register once, move three times, and never tell anyone. If your address on file is stale, the polling place you are sent to may not be yours.

**Do not assume another office did it for you.** In many states a license transaction offers registration; in Trenton that assumption is riskier, because the office serving the town closed in 2023 and those errands now happen elsewhere — see [licensing a vehicle](/how-to/license-your-vehicle/). Confirm with the clerk rather than assuming it traveled with a form you signed somewhere else.

**Students count as a special case.** Anyone living in Trenton to attend college has a choice of registration address to make deliberately, not by default, and the answer changes which local races they vote in.

## What to ask when you call

1. What is the registration deadline for the next election, and what is the deadline after that?
2. What identification will I be asked for at the polls, and what happens if I arrive without it?
3. Which polling place does my exact address feed, and has it changed recently?
4. What are my options if I cannot vote in person, and what are the deadlines for each?
5. How do I confirm my registration is active before election day rather than at the door?

Question five is the one people skip and then regret. A registration you have confirmed once, in writing, is worth more on election morning than any page on this site.

## If something goes wrong at the polls

Ask for the election judge and ask what your options are before you leave. A provisional ballot is generally better than no ballot, and leaving the polling place is the one move that cannot be undone. Afterwards, take the problem back to the County Clerk's office — that is where the record lives and where a fix is made for next time.
