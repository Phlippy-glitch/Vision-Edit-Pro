## Three parties, and knowing which one you want

**The hauler drives the truck. The utility sends the bill. The City sets the contract.** Nearly every wasted call about Trenton trash comes from taking a question to the wrong one of those three.

We print no rate, no pickup day and no holiday schedule on this page, because none has been checked with anyone. What we can give you is who does what, what to have ready, and the questions that get the rest in one call.

## Who currently does what

The City of Trenton's own pages describe residential trash collection and recycling as city-contracted services, with [Rapid Removal Disposal](/place/rapid-removal-disposal/) reported as the trash hauler and [Hope Haven Industries](/place/hope-haven-industries/) as the recycling collector. Residents are billed through [Trenton Municipal Utilities](/place/trenton-municipal-utilities/) rather than by the hauler.

**Those names have a shelf life.** A collection contract is a contract, and contracts are re-bid. Anyone reading this a year from now should treat the names as a lead and ask who holds each contract today. That is a [City of Trenton](/place/city-of-trenton/) question.

The City's material also states that the recycling fee is billed to every utility account with water service, used or not. New residents find that out on a statement more often than on a phone call, which is a reason to ask about it early.

## Route your call correctly

- **A missed or partial pickup, a damaged cart, a spill.** Call the hauler. They have the route sheet and the driver.
- **A charge you do not recognise, a final bill, a service start or stop.** Call the utility billing office. The hauler cannot change a line on a bill it did not issue.
- **Who holds the contract, what it covers, why the fee exists at all.** That is a City matter. Municipal codes are where the residential collection requirement usually lives, and Trenton's code is published online — linked below.

If you are outside city limits, none of the above is automatic. Rural addresses often arrange collection privately, which means a subscription with a hauler rather than a line on a city utility bill. Ask before you assume the city arrangement follows the mailing address.

## What to have ready

- Your utility account number and the service address.
- Your collection day, if you know it, and the time you set the cart out.
- A photograph of the setout, taken the morning of the miss. It settles "it was not out" in one message.
- The date of the statement, if the question is a charge.

## What goes wrong here

**Holiday weeks shift the route.** Collection days move around holidays, and the shifted day is announced by the hauler or the city rather than by us. Assume the schedule changes and check the week before.

**Set-out time is earlier than people think.** A cart placed out after the truck has passed reads to the route as a cart that was never out. If a miss happens twice, ask what time the route reaches your street.

**Not everything goes in the cart.** Bulky items, appliances, tyres, electronics, construction debris, paint and yard waste are commonly excluded, or handled separately. Ask what your service covers before you drag a sofa to the kerb.

**Recycling rules are local and specific.** What one collector accepts, another rejects, and a contaminated load can be sent to landfill. Ask for the accepted-materials list from the collector rather than working from a list you learned in another town.

**Moving out.** Stopping utility service and stopping collection may be the same request or two requests. Ask which, or you may be billed past your last day.

## What to ask when you call

1. Which day is my collection, and how do holiday weeks change it?
2. What time does the route reach my street, and by when does the cart need to be out?
3. What does my service include, and what is excluded — bulky items, appliances, yard waste, electronics?
4. What is on the accepted list for recycling, and what contaminates a load?
5. Is the recycling charge on my utility account optional, and if it is not, who set that rule?

Question five is the one to ask in the right order: find out who sets the charge before you ask anyone to remove it. The office that bills you may have no authority over the fee it is billing.
